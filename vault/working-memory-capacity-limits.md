# Working-Memory Capacity Limits

Working memory keeps currently relevant information available while people reason, comprehend, or plan. Its capacity is limited, but a capacity estimate counts **meaningful chunks under specified conditions**, not words, cards, controls, or arbitrary visual groups.

## What the Limit Means

A research overview argues for roughly **3–5 central chunks in young adults** when rehearsal, opportunistic grouping, and sensory traces are constrained. Familiar repeat-back tasks can produce the older **7±2** estimate because they allow supporting strategies; that does not make seven a clean estimate of unaided central storage.

A learned pair can become one chunk containing two words. A border around two unfamiliar facts does not establish the same compression. Experts and novices may therefore need to retain different numbers of independent units even when looking at identical material.

## Evidence

The overview describes convergence across differently controlled tasks:

- After word pairs were learned to **100% pair-completion accuracy**, participants repeating a word to suppress rehearsal recalled about **3 units**, whether each unit was a singleton or learned pair. Recall did not require correct serial position, so this is not proof that order and binding have no cost.
- A visual-array comparison estimated about **1.5 stored items for 7–8-year-olds**, versus about **3.0 for 11–12-year-olds and college students**. Comparable selective allocation in **4-item arrays** showed that lower storage cannot always be explained by poorer filtering.
- After masking sensory traces, adults retained about **4 visual items** when attending only vision, or about **4 items total** when attending vision and audition together. Without the mask, two modalities performed better than one: the result does not mean additional channels never help.

The overview reports no sample sizes, confidence intervals, standardized effects, or pooled estimate for these experiments. Its exact biological explanation remains open.

A separate web-guideline synthesis rated designing for working-memory limitations **4/5 in relative importance and 5/5 in evidence strength**. It recommends co-visible comparisons and avoiding cross-page retention. These are panel-appraised evidence ratings, not measured effect sizes; the synthesis does not establish a fixed display-item limit.

## Visual Selection Is Not Comprehensive Storage

A separate perception survey distinguishes rapid ensemble summaries from retained
individual detail. Cited dot-display experiments supported mean-size judgments
despite poor judgments about whether an individual size appeared; examples
achieved **at least 75% accuracy for diameter differences of 8–12%**. This is
aggregate perception, not a capacity estimate or proof that each member is stored.

In cited search experiments, previewing **4–8 arbitrary color–shape conjunctions**
for up to **300 milliseconds** offered no search advantage. That does not show
that all prior viewing is useless: other cited studies found faster search in
repeated layouts without conscious awareness. Explicit recall, implicit
memory-guided selection, and storage available for comparison are different
outcomes. The survey reports **no new experiment or pooled estimate**.

## Interruption and Suspended Context

A **50-person** experiment compared forced secondary work mid-task with delivery
at task boundaries. Within the mid-task group, interrupted trials required
**3–27%** more primary-work time across task categories, excluding secondary-work
duration; the overall between-group timing effect on primary time was not
significant. Group-level errors and affect also worsened with mid-task delivery.

Recovering suspended goals and allocating resources in anticipation of
interruptions are plausible explanations, not measured memory mechanisms. The
experiment did **not measure working-memory capacity or allocation** and does not
show that interruption reduces a person's chunk capacity by a particular amount.
[Interruption costs](/vault/interruption-costs.md) concern task continuity as well
as concurrent retention.

## Operational Context Is More Than Item Storage

A process-control theory paper distinguishes an operator's evolving working
storage—predictions, decisions, and expectations about the process—from a simple
short-term item store. Seeing current readings does not instantly restore the
understanding accumulated through involvement. Its observation that operators
arrived **a quarter to half an hour** before takeover to gain a feel for operation
is **practice observation, not a controlled recovery-time estimate**.

This adds a context-acquisition boundary, not another measurement of chunk
capacity. The paper supplies no new participant experiment or quantified memory
effect. [Out-of-the-loop supervision](/vault/out-of-the-loop-supervision.md)
concerns whether people remain able to interpret and act, not merely whether
enough values fit in storage.

## Practical Use

- Budget what must be remembered concurrently: values, instructions, criteria, and intermediate results can compete for the same limited availability.
- Keep needed state inspectable through [recognition over recall](/vault/recognition-over-recall.md) and [juxtaposability for comparison](/vault/juxtaposability-for-comparison.md).
- Help people understand meaningful relationships rather than assuming visual grouping creates learned chunks.
- Reduce distracting material and retention demand separately; improved filtering need not enlarge storage.
- Avoid assuming rehearsal will remain available during concurrent language processing, interruption, or unpredictable sequences.
- Support reinspection and persistent before/after comparison; salience and gaze
  do not establish that individual details remain available across views.
- Preserve goals and intermediate state through interruptions. Reduced memory
  demands also support [cognitive accessibility user needs](/vault/cognitive-accessibility-user-needs.md),
  without assuming these general studies measure disability-specific outcomes.
- **Extrapolation from process control to software or AI-agent supervision:**
  retain the goal, consequential decision history, and relevant trajectory so a
  returning reviewer can rebuild context. Current values alone may be insufficient;
  the source does not measure modern agent takeover times.

These are design applications of the evidence, not tested guarantees for a particular arrangement.

## Limitations

The **3–5** range is neither an invariant score for every person nor the capacity of every memory system. Age, familiarity, task structure, sensory support, and measurement assumptions matter. Visible choices need not all be memorized: neither four nor seven is a universal menu-size rule.

A later theoretical review contrasts discrete item limits with flexibly distributed resources whose representational quality determines performance. This challenges a literal four-slot architecture without constituting a failed replication of the cited demonstrations. The broad limitation of working memory is better supported here than any settled mechanism or universal prediction.

Missed visual changes can reflect decay, inaccessible storage, incompatible
representations, or failure to compare; the perception survey does not isolate
these alternatives. Its findings do not settle a fixed item capacity. Likewise,
forced interruption effects do not identify memory as their cause or establish
the costs of user-controlled notifications. Externalizing state is a design
inference, not a quantified guarantee from either source.

The process-control argument does not identify a biological storage mechanism or
measure a display intervention. Its operational-context observations cannot be
converted into a universal waiting period, a larger chunk estimate, or proof
that external records instantly restore understanding.
