# Working-Memory Capacity Limits

Working memory keeps currently relevant information available while people reason, comprehend, or plan. Its capacity is limited, but a capacity estimate counts **meaningful chunks under specified conditions**, not words, cards, controls, or arbitrary visual groups.

## What the Limit Means

A research overview argues for roughly **3–5 central chunks in young adults** when rehearsal, opportunistic grouping, and sensory traces are constrained. Familiar repeat-back tasks can produce the older **7±2** estimate because they allow supporting strategies; that does not make seven a clean estimate of unaided central storage.

A learned pair can become one chunk containing two words. A border around two unfamiliar facts does not establish the same compression. Experts and novices may therefore need to retain different numbers of independent units even when looking at identical material.

## Evidence

The overview describes convergence across differently controlled tasks:

- After word pairs were learned to **100% pair-completion accuracy**, participants repeating a word to suppress rehearsal recalled about **3 units**, whether each unit was a singleton or learned pair. Recall did not require correct serial position, so this is not proof that order and binding have no cost.
- A visual-array comparison estimated about **1.5 stored items for 7–8-year-olds**, versus about **3.0 for 11–12-year-olds and college students**. Comparable selective allocation in **4-item arrays** showed that lower storage cannot always be explained by poorer filtering.
- After masking sensory traces, adults retained about **4 visual items** when attending only vision, or about **4 items total** when attending vision and audition together. Without the mask, two modalities performed better than one: the result does not mean additional channels never help.

The overview reports no sample sizes, confidence intervals, standardized effects, or pooled estimate for these experiments. Its exact biological explanation remains open.

A separate web-guideline synthesis rated designing for working-memory limitations **4/5 in relative importance and 5/5 in evidence strength**. It recommends co-visible comparisons and avoiding cross-page retention. These are panel-appraised evidence ratings, not measured effect sizes; the synthesis does not establish a fixed display-item limit.

## Practical Use

- Budget what must be remembered concurrently: values, instructions, criteria, and intermediate results can compete for the same limited availability.
- Keep needed state inspectable through [recognition over recall](/vault/recognition-over-recall.md) and [juxtaposability for comparison](/vault/juxtaposability-for-comparison.md).
- Help people understand meaningful relationships rather than assuming visual grouping creates learned chunks.
- Reduce distracting material and retention demand separately; improved filtering need not enlarge storage.
- Avoid assuming rehearsal will remain available during concurrent language processing, interruption, or unpredictable sequences.

These are design applications of the evidence, not tested guarantees for a particular arrangement.

## Limitations

The **3–5** range is neither an invariant score for every person nor the capacity of every memory system. Age, familiarity, task structure, sensory support, and measurement assumptions matter. Visible choices need not all be memorized: neither four nor seven is a universal menu-size rule.

A later theoretical review contrasts discrete item limits with flexibly distributed resources whose representational quality determines performance. This challenges a literal four-slot architecture without constituting a failed replication of the cited demonstrations. The broad limitation of working memory is better supported here than any settled mechanism or universal prediction.
