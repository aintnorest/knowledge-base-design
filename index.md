# Knowledge Base

## Dossiers

* [Understanding WCAG 2.2](/dossiers/understanding-wcag-2-2.md) — WCAG 2.2's informative commentary explains accessibility as preserving perception, operation, comprehension, and assistive-technology access, while admitting that testable conformance thresholds and exceptions do not guarantee usability for every disabled person.
* [Research-Based Web Design & Usability Guidelines](/dossiers/research-based-web-design-usability-guidelines.md) — A 2006 synthesis separates the judged importance of 209 web-design guidelines from their research support, but its evidence ratings are contextual expert assessments and many desktop-era prescriptions have aged.
* [Enhancing the Explanatory Power of Usability Heuristics](/dossiers/enhancing-explanatory-power-usability-heuristics.md) — A single-rater analysis of 249 existing usability problems synthesizes nine heuristics from 101 principles, but explanatory coverage is not evidence that the new set detects more problems.
* [The Evaluator Effect: A Chilling Fact about Usability Evaluation Methods](/dossiers/evaluator-effect-usability-evaluation-methods.md) — Eleven studies show that evaluators using the same usability method often find substantially different problems, but agreement measures establish reliability rather than real-world validity or exhaustive coverage.
* [First Principles of Interaction Design (Revised & Expanded)](/dossiers/first-principles-of-interaction-design.md) — Tognazzini argues for visible, forgiving, user-controlled interfaces and evaluates simplicity through human productivity, but most detailed rules are practitioner guidance rather than traceable experimental findings.
* [A Century of Gestalt Psychology in Visual Perception I. Perceptual Grouping and Figure-Ground Organization](/dossiers/century-gestalt-psychology-grouping-figure-ground.md) — A century-spanning review shows how grouping and border ownership emerge from interacting visual cues, while rejecting universal cue rankings and a single exclusively bottom-up stage of organization.
* [The Magical Mystery Four: How is Working Memory Capacity Limited, and Why?](/dossiers/magical-mystery-four-working-memory-capacity.md) — Cowan synthesizes evidence for roughly three to five central working-memory chunks when rehearsal, regrouping, and sensory support are controlled, without establishing a universal display-item limit or a settled biological mechanism.
* [Fitts' Law as a Research and Design Tool in Human-Computer Interaction](/dossiers/fitts-law-research-design-tool-hci.md) — MacKenzie strengthens pointing-time modeling with the Shannon formulation and effective target width, while showing that heterogeneous tasks and error policies prevent published device throughputs from serving as universal design constants.
* [Response Time in Man-Computer Conversational Transactions](/dossiers/response-time-man-computer-conversational-transactions.md) — Miller proposes task-specific latency limits based on expectation, short-term memory, and psychological closure, but explicitly presents the thresholds as calculated guesses requiring real-task validation rather than experimental findings.
* [Aesthetics and Apparent Usability: Empirically Assessing Cultural and Methodological Issues](/dossiers/aesthetics-and-apparent-usability.md) — Three Israeli ATM-layout studies replicate a strong association between beauty and expected ease of use, but measure neither actual usability nor persistence after interaction.
* [The Role of Visual Complexity and Prototypicality Regarding First Impression of Websites: Working Towards Understanding Aesthetic Judgments](/dossiers/visual-complexity-prototypicality-website-first-impressions.md) — Two masked screenshot studies find the strongest aesthetic first impressions for visually simple, prototypical company websites even at 17 milliseconds, without testing interaction or long-term preference.
* [Predicting Users’ First Impressions of Website Aesthetics With a Quantification of Perceived Visual Complexity and Colorfulness](/dossiers/predicting-website-aesthetics-first-impressions.md) — Screenshot-derived complexity and colorfulness models with demographic terms explain 48% of observed 500-millisecond appeal-rating variance, but do not establish causal design rules or validated performance on new populations and sites.
* [How Do Users Evaluate the Credibility of Web Sites? A Study with Over 2,500 Participants](/dossiers/how-users-evaluate-web-credibility.md) — In a large live-site comparison study, design look appeared in 46.1% of coded credibility comments, demonstrating reported prominence rather than its causal share of trust or information accuracy.
* [Dark Patterns at Scale: Findings from a Crawl of 11K Shopping Websites](/dossiers/dark-patterns-at-scale-shopping-websites.md) — A crawl of 11,286 shopping websites documents 1,818 dark-pattern instances and a third-party supply ecosystem, but its text-centered discovery pipeline yields a lower bound rather than population prevalence or measured consumer harm.
* [The Dark (Patterns) Side of UX Design](/dossiers/dark-patterns-side-of-ux-design.md) — A qualitative analysis of 118 practitioner-circulated artifacts reorganizes dark patterns into five design strategies and frames them as conflicts between user and shareholder value, without measuring prevalence, designer intent, or behavioral effects.
* [F-Shaped Pattern of Reading on the Web: Misunderstood, But Still Relevant (Even on Mobile)](/dossiers/f-shaped-pattern-reading-web-content.md) — NN/g observes top-heavy and reading-start-edge-heavy scanning again on desktop and mobile, but the F-shape is conditional and the public report does not quantify its prevalence or the efficacy of proposed antidotes.
* [Flat UI Elements Attract Less Attention and Cause Uncertainty](/dossiers/flat-ui-elements-attract-less-attention.md) — A 71-user desktop eye-tracking experiment finds weak-signifier pages take 22% longer and require 25% more fixations in targeted findability tasks, not necessarily in complete real-world workflows.
* [Affordance, Conventions, and Design](/dossiers/affordance-conventions-and-design.md) — Norman separates possible action from perceived action and learned screen conventions, but offers conceptual clarification rather than an empirical test of their usability effects.
* [Cognitive Dimensions of Information Artefacts: a Tutorial](/dossiers/cognitive-dimensions-information-artefacts-tutorial.md) — Cognitive dimensions expose activity-dependent tradeoffs in information artefacts, but the framework is a discussion vocabulary rather than a validated overall usability score.
* [Microsoft Inclusive 101 Guidebook](/dossiers/microsoft-inclusive-101-guidebook.md) — Inclusive design treats exclusion as a mismatch between people and environments and uses disability-led insight to widen participation, but this guidebook does not establish measured outcome gains or reproducible population estimates.
* [Magic Ink: Information Software and the Graphical Interface](/dossiers/magic-ink-information-software.md) — Information software can reduce context-setting interaction through comparison-oriented graphics and inference from environment and history, but Victor's essay and simulated predictor do not establish universal benefits or resolve privacy and inference-error costs.
* [Heuristic evaluation of user interfaces](/dossiers/heuristic-evaluation-of-user-interfaces.md) — Four small-interface experiments show independent evaluators find complementary usability problems and simulated unions substantially improve coverage, without establishing exhaustive detection or validity in actual use.
* [The Cognitive Walkthrough Method: A Practitioner's Guide](/dossiers/cognitive-walkthrough-practitioners-guide.md) — A theory-based inspection explains how users learn through goal-directed exploration and exposes knowledge assumptions at each action, but its simplified third version had no empirical validation in this technical report.
* [Direct Manipulation Interfaces](/dossiers/direct-manipulation-interfaces.md) — A conceptual account distinguishes semantic and articulatory distance from direct engagement, explaining why manipulable representations can reduce translation effort without making every task easier or replacing abstraction.
* [Making Content Usable for People with Cognitive and Learning Disabilities](/dossiers/making-content-usable-cognitive-learning-disabilities.md) — W3C's supplemental guidance treats independent task completion as a cognitive-accessibility goal through familiar interfaces, clear content, recoverable errors, and reduced memory demands, without making these patterns WCAG conformance requirements or uniformly validated treatments.
* [The WebAIM Million — The 2026 report on the accessibility of the top 1,000,000 home pages](/dossiers/webaim-million-2026.md) — Automated WAVE analysis of one million popular home pages detected likely WCAG A/AA failures on 95.9% in February 2026, reversing recent prevalence improvements without establishing full conformance or the causes of technology-related differences.
* [Positive display polarity is advantageous for both younger and older adults](/dossiers/positive-display-polarity-younger-older-adults.md) — Dark-on-light displays improve visual acuity and proofreading accuracy in younger and older adults with normal or corrected vision, but the study excludes clinically relevant eye disease and does not establish a fatigue benefit.
* [Effects of ambient illumination, contrast polarity, and letter size on text legibility under glance-like reading](/dossiers/ambient-illumination-contrast-polarity-letter-size.md) — Brief word-recognition thresholds worsen for white-on-black text in near-darkness, especially at the smaller tested size, while daylight-like illumination removes the statistically significant polarity difference in a selected working-adult sample.
* [How bold can we be? The impact of adjusting font grade on readability in light and dark polarities](/dossiers/how-bold-can-we-be-font-grade-polarity.md) — Two remote studies find faster reading in light mode and no detected paragraph-fluency effect of font grade in one tested variable typeface, while preference remains balanced and reporting inconsistencies limit precise interpretation.
* [Dark Mode: How Users Think About It and Issues to Avoid](/dossiers/dark-mode-users-issues.md) — NN/g's mobile survey and qualitative testing suggest users expect dark mode to follow system settings yet often tolerate unsupported pages, while evidence for comfort and power savings is conditional and poor conversions can erase interface information.
* [The Cost of Cool: Typographic Style Legibility in Reading at a Glance](/dossiers/cost-of-cool-typographic-style-legibility.md) — A 31-person masked lexical-decision experiment finds lowercase and condensed Frutiger require longer exposure than uppercase and regular-width text, with case penalties amplified at smaller sizes, not a demonstrated cost of aesthetic appeal or real-world multitasking.
* [Psychophysics of reading. XV: Font effects in normal and low vision](/dossiers/psychophysics-of-reading-font-effects.md) — A printed-chart study of 50 normally sighted and 42 low-vision readers finds Courier-Bold supports smaller readable text than Times-Roman, while maximum-speed advantages differ by vision group and cannot be attributed to monospacing alone.
* [Letter case and text legibility in normal and low vision](/dossiers/letter-case-text-legibility.md) — Small-sample Arial experiments find uppercase supports smaller identification thresholds and faster reading near acuity limits, while its speed advantage disappears in a two-person large-text condition and does not establish a universal preference for capitals.
* [Aesthetic response to color combinations: preference, harmony, and similarity](/dossiers/aesthetic-response-color-combinations.md) — Four repeated-measures experiments distinguish preference for a color pair, its harmony, and preference for its foreground color, with opposite hue-contrast effects bounded to simple displays and one 48-person sample.
* [Practical Rules for Using Color in Charts](/dossiers/practical-rules-using-color-in-charts.md) — Stephen Few recommends assigning chart color to communication goals, ordered data, and selective emphasis, supported by visual demonstrations and practitioner rationale rather than controlled performance studies.
* [Designing accessible color systems](/dossiers/designing-accessible-color-systems.md) — Stripe describes aligning palette lightness across hues to make contrast predictable while retaining color identity, but its 2019 case study demonstrates selected contrast checks rather than universal accessibility or measured perceptual equality.
* [Attention and Visual Memory in Visualization and Computer Graphics](/dossiers/attention-visual-memory-visualization.md) — A perception survey explains how feature contrast, goals, and memory jointly govern visual search and missed changes, while warning that laboratory pop-out findings do not by themselves specify reliable visualization encodings.
* [Moticons: Detection, Distraction and Task](/dossiers/moticons-detection-distraction-task.md) — Small dual-task experiments find oscillating icons easier to detect than color or shape changes, especially far from the task, but traveling motion draws more subjective distraction and primary-task harm was not measured.
* [On the need for attention-aware systems: Measuring effects of interruption on task performance, error rate, and affective state](/dossiers/attention-aware-systems-interruption-effects.md) — A 50-person experiment finds mid-task interruptions increase within-person completion time and between-group errors, annoyance, and anxiety relative to task-boundary delivery, but it tests forced modal tasks rather than freely managed notifications.
* [What is beautiful is usable](/dossiers/what-is-beautiful-is-usable.md) — A 124-person ATM-simulation experiment finds appearance predicts post-use perceived usability despite deliberately slower interaction, but not better task performance or inevitable persistence across systems.
* [Assessing dimensions of perceived visual aesthetics of web sites](/dossiers/perceived-visual-aesthetics-web-sites.md) — Four scale-development studies distinguish orderly classical aesthetics from creative expressive aesthetics, with stronger perceived-usability correlations for classical qualities but no causal preference or performance test.
* [Facets of visual aesthetics](/dossiers/facets-of-visual-aesthetics.md) — VisAWI construction and validation support four related website-aesthetics facets under a general factor, while incomplete divergent validity and untested cross-cultural and test–retest stability limit universal interpretation.
* [Quantifying Visual Preferences Around the World](/dossiers/quantifying-visual-preferences-around-the-world.md) — A large international screenshot study finds demographic differences in preferred complexity and colorfulness, but its observational first-impression models do not establish cultural causes or universal personalized-design benefits.
* [Humans and Automation: Use, Misuse, Disuse, Abuse](/dossiers/humans-and-automation-use-misuse-disuse-abuse.md) — A review distinguishes automation use, overreliance, underuse, and design or management abuse, showing that reliability, workload, feedback, and alarm base rates interact rather than making human supervision uniformly effective.
* [A Model for Types and Levels of Human Interaction with Automation](/dossiers/types-and-levels-of-automation.md) — A qualitative framework separates four automation functions and their levels of authority, recommending choices based on human performance, reliability, and consequences rather than a universal optimum or a single autonomy score.
* [Trust in Automation: Designing for Appropriate Reliance](/dossiers/trust-in-automation-appropriate-reliance.md) — An integrative review argues that trust should match automation capability at the relevant function and time, while reliance also depends on self-confidence, workload, engagement costs, and context rather than trust alone.
* [Ironies of Automation](/dossiers/ironies-of-automation.md) — A process-control theory paper argues that automating routine work can erode the skills and current understanding needed for exceptional takeover, so greater automation may require stronger human support rather than eliminate it.
* [Guidelines for Human-AI Interaction](/dossiers/guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction.md) — Four iterative validation phases establish the inspectability and broad relevance of 18 human-AI design guidelines, not causal improvements in user performance or a complete ethical checklist.
* [Principles of Mixed-Initiative User Interfaces](/dossiers/principles-of-mixed-initiative-user-interfaces.md) — A decision-theoretic design account combines autonomous assistance with direct manipulation by weighing user-goal uncertainty and action costs, illustrated by LookOut rather than a controlled usability comparison.
* [To Trust or to Think: Cognitive Forcing Functions Can Reduce Overreliance on AI in AI-assisted Decision-making](/dossiers/cognitive-forcing-functions-overreliance-on-ai.md) — A 199-person nutrition experiment finds cognitive forcing reduces acceptance of incorrect AI ingredient choices but not overall team accuracy, with larger benefits for high-need-for-cognition participants and subjective tradeoffs.
* [Does the Whole Exceed its Parts? The Effect of AI Explanations on Complementary Team Performance](/dossiers/ai-explanations-complementary-team-performance.md) — Three crowdworker tasks produce human–AI accuracy above either partner alone, but local explanations add no significant accuracy benefit beyond recommendations with confidence and can increase acceptance of wrong advice.
* [Comparison of Four Primary Methods for Coordinating the Interruption of People in Human–Computer Interaction](/dossiers/coordinating-interruption-four-methods.md) — A 36-person dual-task experiment favors user-negotiated interruptions overall and immediate delivery for time-critical secondary work, but compares minimal implementations around a continuous, hidden, nonpausable game.
* [Disruption and Recovery of Computing Tasks: Field Study, Analysis, and Directions](/dossiers/disruption-recovery-computing-tasks.md) — Two weeks of workplace logging associate communication-alert diversions with roughly 8–10 minutes away and a further 11–17-minute resumption phase, but window-based observational proxies cannot isolate cognitive recovery or prove alerts caused the whole delay.
* [The Cost of Interrupted Work: More Speed and Stress](/dossiers/cost-of-interrupted-work.md) — A 48-person email experiment finds interrupted primary work is completed faster with no detected error or politeness penalty but greater stress and effort, without establishing faster total workflows or equivalence between interruption contexts.
* [Designing Glanceable Peripheral Displays](/dossiers/designing-glanceable-peripheral-displays.md) — A literature-informed exploration of 30 email-display designs and interviews with nine designers proposes meaningful, abstract, distinct, consistent encodings for quick interpretation, but does not measure glance time or multitasking performance.

## Vault

* [Aesthetic–Usability Effect](/vault/aesthetic-usability-effect.md) — Attractive interfaces can appear easier to use, but the association does not establish better performance or an inevitable persistence after interaction.
* [Appropriate Reliance on Automation](/vault/appropriate-reliance-on-automation.md) — Reliance should fit automation capability, action consequences, and available alternatives, because higher trust or more persuasive explanations do not necessarily improve joint decisions.
* [Automated Accessibility Testing Limits](/vault/automated-accessibility-testing-limits.md) — Automated accessibility tests expose a subset of likely failures, but zero detected errors does not establish accessibility or conformance, and prevalence estimates remain bounded by the pages and checks sampled.
* [Classical and Expressive Aesthetics](/vault/classical-and-expressive-aesthetics.md) — Perceived interface aesthetics includes related judgments of order, expression, color, and craftsmanship, but validated dimensions describe appreciation rather than prescribe design changes or guarantee usability.
* [Cognitive Accessibility User Needs](/vault/cognitive-accessibility-user-needs.md) — Cognitive accessibility supports independent task completion by reducing avoidable comprehension, memory, attention, and recovery demands, but consensus patterns do not establish universal usability or measured treatment effects.
* [Cognitive Dimensions Tradeoffs](/vault/cognitive-dimensions-tradeoffs.md) — Cognitive dimensions describe activity-dependent structural burdens in information artefacts, making redesign tradeoffs explicit rather than reducing usability to a single score.
* [Cognitive Walkthrough](/vault/cognitive-walkthrough.md) — Task-bound reasoning exposes knowledge assumptions in learning by exploration, but correct-path inspection neither predicts error trajectories nor guarantees evaluator agreement or comprehensive usability coverage.
* [Color Coding for Status](/vault/color-coding-for-status.md) — Color can distinguish categories and states efficiently, but reliable meaning requires discrimination under actual viewing conditions, non-color cues, and selective use of emphasis.
* [Color Combination Preference and Harmony](/vault/color-combination-preference-and-harmony.md) — Whole-pair liking, harmony, similarity, and foreground liking respond differently to hue relationships, lightness contrast, and component preferences, so no single aesthetic judgment defines a universally good color combination.
* [Color Contrast Minimum](/vault/color-contrast-minimum.md) — Minimum luminance contrast protects access to necessary text and graphics, but a passing ratio does not establish legibility or replace non-color cues and semantics.
* [Consistency and Convention](/vault/consistency-and-convention.md) — Stable meanings, learned action consequences, and shared conventions let people reuse knowledge, but familiarity is audience-specific and does not require uniform appearance.
* [Context Inferred over Interaction](/vault/context-inferred-over-interaction.md) — Information software can reduce repeated context-setting by inferring a useful starting view from environment and history, organizing it around decision questions, and keeping correction inexpensive.
* [Contrast Polarity and Legibility](/vault/contrast-polarity-and-legibility.md) — Dark-on-light text often improves recognition or reading for normal and corrected vision, but the advantage depends on task and illumination and does not establish greater comfort or suit every visual condition.
* [Deceptive Pattern Attributes](/vault/deceptive-pattern-attributes.md) — Five overlapping attributes distinguish unequal choice burden, concealed influence, false beliefs, hidden information, and restricted alternatives without treating every persuasive design as deception.
* [Disability as Interaction Mismatch](/vault/disability-as-interaction-mismatch.md) — Locating exclusion in the encounter between human capabilities and environmental demands identifies changeable barriers without equating permanent disability with temporary or situational constraints.
* [Error Prevention and Recovery](/vault/error-prevention-and-recovery.md) — Prevent foreseeable mistakes and make unavoidable failures identifiable, correctable, and recoverable without discarding the user's work or task context.
* [Evaluator Effect](/vault/evaluator-effect.md) — Independent evaluators using the same usability method often identify different problems, so agreement, discovery coverage, and real-world validity must be assessed separately.
* [Figure–Ground Organization](/vault/figure-ground-organization.md) — Figure–ground organization assigns shared borders to shaped surfaces through context-sensitive image cues, familiarity, and attention.
* [Fitts's Law and Pointing Time](/vault/fitts-law-pointing-time.md) — Pointing time depends on distance relative to target tolerance, while effective width normalizes the performed speed–accuracy tradeoff and fitted coefficients remain task-specific.
* [Glanceable Peripheral Displays](/vault/glanceable-peripheral-displays.md) — Compact, meaningful encodings can support quick interpretation once inspected, but expert-rated glanceability and peripheral event detection do not establish improved multitasking or comprehension.
* [Gulfs of Execution and Evaluation](/vault/gulfs-of-execution-and-evaluation.md) — Usability depends on bridging goals to actions and system state back to goals, but reducing surface translation cannot repair a task-level mismatch or remove domain difficulty.
* [Heuristic Evaluation](/vault/heuristic-evaluation.md) — Compact usability principles guide inspection toward plausible problems, but independent aggregation improves known-problem coverage without certifying validity or exhaustive discovery.
* [Importance versus Evidence Strength](/vault/importance-versus-evidence-strength.md) — A design recommendation's implementation priority and the strength of its supporting evidence are separate judgments, with obligations supplying an independent constraint.
* [Interruption Coordination](/vault/interruption-coordination.md) — Coordinate secondary demands through immediate, negotiated, mediated, or scheduled delivery according to urgency and attention, without treating one tested implementation or task boundary as a universal optimum.
* [Interruption Costs](/vault/interruption-costs.md) — Interruptions can alter primary performance, affect, and the path back to sustained work, but faster active work and long observed return delays establish neither low burden nor universal causal recovery costs.
* [Juxtaposability for Comparison](/vault/juxtaposability-for-comparison.md) — Making related information viewable together supports comparison without repeated retrieval and memory-dependent switching between representations.
* [Levels of Automation](/vault/levels-of-automation.md) — Automation is a profile of function-specific authority rather than a single autonomy score, and suitable levels depend on human performance, reliability, and consequences rather than a universal optimum.
* [Minimum Target Size](/vault/minimum-target-size.md) — Minimum active target dimensions and spacing reduce precision demands, but accessibility conformance floors do not guarantee easy or safe acquisition.
* [Mixed-Initiative Interaction](/vault/mixed-initiative-interaction.md) — Share initiative through context-sensitive acting, asking, and waiting while preserving user control and independent judgment, without treating confidence or delayed advice as universal performance guarantees.
* [Motion Cues: Attention and Distraction](/vault/motion-cues-attention-and-distraction.md) — Motion can improve peripheral event detection relative to static changes, but trajectory, competing tasks, and successive events bound its reliability and distraction cost.
* [Out-of-the-Loop Supervision](/vault/out-of-the-loop-supervision.md) — Automating ordinary work can weaken monitoring, current understanding, and practiced skills needed for exceptional takeover, but the risk depends on competing tasks and the recovery role rather than automation alone.
* [Perceptual Grouping Cues](/vault/perceptual-grouping-cues.md) — Perceived membership and contour unity arise from interacting spatial, appearance, motion, and surface cues rather than a universal hierarchy of Gestalt laws.
* [Perceptually Uniform Color Scales](/vault/perceptually-uniform-color-scales.md) — Perceptual color models make lightness relationships across hues more predictable, but palette structure must match the message and remain bounded by approximate uniformity, hue-specific chroma, and device gamut.
* [Preattentive Features](/vault/preattentive-features.md) — Distinctive visual features can support rapid search, but their advantage depends on contrast, distractors, feature combinations, and goals rather than guaranteeing attention or memory.
* [Recognition over Recall](/vault/recognition-over-recall.md) — Keeping relevant actions and task context perceivable reduces dependence on remembering information that is no longer available.
* [Response-Time Thresholds](/vault/response-time-thresholds.md) — Acceptable delay depends on the response function and the user's unfinished task, while familiar numerical thresholds are guidance rather than universal experimentally established limits.
* [Signifier Strength](/vault/signifier-strength.md) — Perceivable cues help people recognize meaningful actions and expected outcomes, but cue strength must be distinguished from real affordance, perceived affordance, and learned convention.
* [Text Alternatives](/vault/text-alternatives.md) — Electronic text preserves the purpose and information of non-text content across sensory presentations, with the alternative shaped by function rather than appearance alone.
* [Typeface Legibility](/vault/typeface-legibility.md) — Letter size, case, width, stroke strength, and font design can shift visual reading thresholds, but their effects depend on acuity reserve and task rather than establishing universal uppercase, monospace, or boldness rules.
* [Visibility of System Status](/vault/visibility-of-system-status.md) — Make current activity, interpretation, and suspended work understandable, while distinguishing visible status from capability, reliability, explanation, and unproven recovery benefits.
* [Visual Complexity and Prototypicality](/vault/visual-complexity-and-prototypicality.md) — Website first-impression appeal depends on perceived complexity and learned category typicality, but very sparse pages need not fare best and audience differences do not provide reliable demographic design rules.
* [Visual Design and Credibility](/vault/visual-design-and-credibility.md) — Visual and social presentation can influence perceived credibility or automation reliance without improving truth or capability, so design should support warranted assessment rather than maximize trust.
* [Web Content Scanning Patterns](/vault/web-content-scanning-patterns.md) — Readers select web content through several goal-dependent scanning strategies, making the F-shape a conditional observation rather than a universal layout rule.
* [Working-Memory Capacity Limits](/vault/working-memory-capacity-limits.md) — Central working memory holds only a few meaningful chunks under controlled conditions, while rebuilding an evolving operational picture is a separate context problem rather than a fixed display-item limit.
